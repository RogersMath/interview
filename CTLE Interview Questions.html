CTLE Instructional Designer Interview Questions

C = commonly asked in ID interviews. B = high-value question that separates strong candidates. Some are both. The note after each question says what the panel is usually testing.

1. Opening and motivation (1–10)

  1. C Tell us about yourself. Testing: a focused 60–90 second story that ends at this role.
  2. C Why do you want this position? Testing: interest in the work, not only the promotion.
  3. C Why do you want to work in the CTLE specifically? Testing: whether you researched their mission and programs.
  4. B You've spent 20 years supporting students directly. Why move to supporting faculty? Testing: a reason that doesn't sound like leaving tutoring behind.
  5. C What do you know about our center and its programs? Testing: preparation.
  6. C What does instructional design mean to you? Testing: a clear, non-jargon definition.
  7. B How would your current colleagues describe how you work? Testing: self-awareness and collaboration.
  8. C What are your greatest strengths for this role? Testing: strengths matched to the job description.
  9. C What's an area you're still developing? Testing: honesty plus a real plan.
  10. B What would you want to accomplish in your first 90 days? Testing: listening before changing things.

2. Design process and backward design (11–25)

  1. C Walk us through how you'd help a faculty member redesign a course. Testing: a collaborative, outcomes-first process.
  2. C What instructional design models do you use? Testing: knowledge plus judgment about when each fits.
  3. B How would you explain backward design to a faculty member who has never heard of it? Testing: plain-language teaching.
  4. B What's the most common alignment problem you'd expect to find in courses? Testing: practical insight.
  5. C How do you write measurable learning outcomes? Testing: observable verbs, ABCD, Bloom's.
  6. B A faculty member's outcomes say students will "understand" the material. What do you do? Testing: tactful rewriting.
  7. C How do you align assessments with learning objectives? Testing: constructive alignment in practice.
  8. B Where does backward design break down, and how do you handle that? Testing: critical thinking, not just advocacy.
  9. C How do you conduct a needs analysis? Testing: gathering evidence before designing.
  10. B How would you design a course when the faculty member has only three weeks before the term starts? Testing: prioritizing under time pressure.
  11. C How do you approach designing an online course differently from a face-to-face one? Testing: interaction, presence, pacing.
  12. C How do you design for hybrid or HyFlex formats? Testing: equivalent experiences across modes.
  13. B How do you decide what content to cut? Testing: essential outcomes over coverage.
  14. C How do you use storyboards, templates, or design documents? Testing: organized workflow.
  15. B How would you design a performance task that shows transfer, not recall? Testing: authentic assessment skill.

3. Working with faculty (26–40)

  1. C How do you build rapport with faculty? Testing: relationship skills.
  2. C Tell us about a time you worked with someone resistant to change. Testing: patience and strategy.
  3. B A senior faculty member says, "I've taught this way for 25 years, and it works." How do you respond? Testing: respect plus evidence.
  4. C How do you handle disagreements with a subject matter expert? Testing: knowing whose call it is.
  5. B Who owns the course, you or the faculty member? Testing: understanding academic freedom.
  6. C How do you manage multiple faculty projects at once? Testing: organization and prioritization.
  7. B How would you work with adjunct faculty who have little time and little pay for course development? Testing: empathy and realistic scope.
  8. C How do you give feedback on a course without making faculty feel judged? Testing: tact.
  9. B A faculty member wants a flashy tool that doesn't serve the outcome. What do you do? Testing: steering back to alignment.
  10. C Tell us about a time you had to explain a technical concept to a non-technical audience. Testing: communication.
  11. B How do you know when a consultation went well? Testing: outcome-focused thinking.
  12. C How do you handle a faculty member who misses deadlines? Testing: accountability without conflict.
  13. B How would you get faculty to attend programming they don't think they need? Testing: motivation and diffusion.
  14. C Describe a successful collaboration you led. Testing: a concrete story with results.
  15. B How do you work with faculty across very different disciplines? Testing: learning outside your expertise.

4. Learning science and pedagogy (41–52)

  1. C What learning theories guide your work? Testing: grounding, not name-dropping.
  2. B Which learning science finding do you wish every faculty member used? Testing: conviction and evidence.
  3. C How do you promote active learning? Testing: practical strategies.
  4. C How do you increase student engagement in online courses? Testing: presence and interaction.
  5. B How do you apply cognitive load theory to course materials? Testing: applied knowledge.
  6. C What is Universal Design for Learning, and how do you apply it? Testing: principles in practice.
  7. B How do you design for first-generation or underprepared students? Testing: equity at a state college.
  8. C How do you design effective feedback into a course? Testing: formative assessment.
  9. B How would you help faculty build retrieval practice and spacing into an existing course? Testing: low-effort, high-impact changes.
  10. C How do you support student motivation? Testing: relevance, autonomy, belonging.
  11. B What does your experience in a learning lab show you about where courses fail students? Testing: your unique perspective.
  12. C What's the difference between andragogy and pedagogy, and does it matter here? Testing: adult learners at a state college.

5. Assessment and evaluation (53–62)

  1. C How do you evaluate whether a course is effective? Testing: evidence beyond satisfaction surveys.
  2. C How do you design rubrics? Testing: clear criteria.
  3. B How would you measure the impact of the Backward Design Academy on students? Testing: program evaluation thinking.
  4. C What evaluation models do you know? Testing: Kirkpatrick, Guskey.
  5. B Course evaluations are positive, but DFW rates are high. What do you investigate? Testing: data reasoning.
  6. C How do you use learning analytics or LMS data? Testing: practical data use.
  7. B How do you balance formative and summative assessment? Testing: assessment design.
  8. C How do you revise a course after it runs? Testing: continuous improvement.
  9. B How would you design a pilot to test whether a course change worked? Testing: basic study design.
  10. C How do you gather student feedback on course design? Testing: student voice.

6. Technology and Canvas (63–72)

  1. C What's your experience with Canvas? Testing: hands-on LMS skill.
  2. C What authoring tools and technologies do you use? Testing: current toolset.
  3. B How do you decide whether a new tool is worth adopting? Testing: outcomes before tools.
  4. C Tell us about a course or module you built from start to finish. Testing: a complete portfolio story.
  5. C How do you create or edit instructional video? Testing: media production basics.
  6. B How do you keep course materials maintainable after you're no longer involved? Testing: sustainability.
  7. C How do you stay current with educational technology? Testing: professional learning habits.
  8. B When would you build a custom interactive instead of using an existing tool? Testing: build-versus-buy judgment.
  9. C How do you organize a Canvas course for easy navigation? Testing: usability.
  10. B What's your view of open educational resources? Testing: cost, quality, and adoption tradeoffs.

7. Accessibility and compliance (73–78)

  1. C How do you ensure courses are accessible? Testing: concrete practices.
  2. C What accessibility standards do you follow? Testing: WCAG 2.1 AA and ADA.
  3. B How would you help faculty fix an inaccessible course with a large backlog of materials? Testing: prioritizing remediation.
  4. C How do you handle copyright and fair use in course materials? Testing: awareness and referral.
  5. B What's the difference between an accessible course and one designed with UDL? Testing: depth.
  6. C What do you know about FERPA as it relates to course design? Testing: privacy basics.

8. AI (79–87)

  1. C How do you see AI affecting instructional design? Testing: balanced, informed view.
  2. B A faculty member wants to ban AI completely. How do you help them? Testing: respect plus practical options.
  3. B A faculty member wants to use AI detectors to catch cheating. What do you say? Testing: evidence and ethics.
  4. C How do you use AI in your own design work? Testing: responsible, verified use.
  5. B How should assessment change now that students have AI? Testing: AI-resilient design.
  6. B How would you design an assignment that teaches students to use AI well? Testing: AI literacy as an outcome.
  7. C What concerns do you have about AI in education? Testing: critical thinking.
  8. B How would you help the CTLE develop AI guidance faculty will actually use? Testing: policy that fits practice.
  9. B Tell us about your work with the SLC AI Work Group. Testing: leadership with evidence of results.

9. Behavioral and situational (88–96)

  1. C Tell us about a time a project didn't go as planned. Testing: accountability and learning.
  2. C Tell us about a time you received critical feedback. Testing: coachability.
  3. C Describe a time you had competing deadlines. Testing: prioritization.
  4. B Tell us about a decision you made using data. Testing: evidence-based judgment.
  5. C Tell us about a time you took initiative without being asked. Testing: proactivity within limits.
  6. B Tell us about a time you changed your mind. Testing: intellectual honesty.
  7. C How do you handle ambiguity in a project? Testing: clarifying questions.
  8. B Your supervisor asks for a project approach you believe is weaker. What do you do? Testing: disagree respectfully, then commit.
  9. C Describe a time you worked on a team with conflicting priorities. Testing: collaboration.

10. Closing (97–100)

  1. B How would you contribute to the CTLE beyond course design? Testing: workshops, programming, and scholarship.
  2. C Where do you see yourself in five years? Testing: commitment to the role.
  3. B Is there anything we haven't asked that you want us to know? Testing: a strong closing point.
  4. C What questions do you have for us? Testing: curiosity and preparation.
