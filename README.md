# CTLE Instructional Designer Interview Questions

**C** = commonly asked in ID interviews. **B** = high-value question that separates strong candidates. Some are both. The note after each question says what the panel is usually testing.

## 1. Opening and motivation (1–10)

1. **C** Tell us about yourself. *Testing: a focused 60–90 second story that ends at this role.*
2. **C** Why do you want this position? *Testing: interest in the work, not only the promotion.*
3. **C** Why do you want to work in the CTLE specifically? *Testing: whether you researched their mission and programs.*
4. **B** You've spent 20 years supporting students directly. Why move to supporting faculty? *Testing: a reason that doesn't sound like leaving tutoring behind.*
5. **C** What do you know about our center and its programs? *Testing: preparation.*
6. **C** What does instructional design mean to you? *Testing: a clear, non-jargon definition.*
7. **B** How would your current colleagues describe how you work? *Testing: self-awareness and collaboration.*
8. **C** What are your greatest strengths for this role? *Testing: strengths matched to the job description.*
9. **C** What's an area you're still developing? *Testing: honesty plus a real plan.*
10. **B** What would you want to accomplish in your first 90 days? *Testing: listening before changing things.*

## 2. Design process and backward design (11–25)

11. **C** Walk us through how you'd help a faculty member redesign a course. *Testing: a collaborative, outcomes-first process.*
12. **C** What instructional design models do you use? *Testing: knowledge plus judgment about when each fits.*
13. **B** How would you explain backward design to a faculty member who has never heard of it? *Testing: plain-language teaching.*
14. **B** What's the most common alignment problem you'd expect to find in courses? *Testing: practical insight.*
15. **C** How do you write measurable learning outcomes? *Testing: observable verbs, ABCD, Bloom's.*
16. **B** A faculty member's outcomes say students will "understand" the material. What do you do? *Testing: tactful rewriting.*
17. **C** How do you align assessments with learning objectives? *Testing: constructive alignment in practice.*
18. **B** Where does backward design break down, and how do you handle that? *Testing: critical thinking, not just advocacy.*
19. **C** How do you conduct a needs analysis? *Testing: gathering evidence before designing.*
20. **B** How would you design a course when the faculty member has only three weeks before the term starts? *Testing: prioritizing under time pressure.*
21. **C** How do you approach designing an online course differently from a face-to-face one? *Testing: interaction, presence, pacing.*
22. **C** How do you design for hybrid or HyFlex formats? *Testing: equivalent experiences across modes.*
23. **B** How do you decide what content to cut? *Testing: essential outcomes over coverage.*
24. **C** How do you use storyboards, templates, or design documents? *Testing: organized workflow.*
25. **B** How would you design a performance task that shows transfer, not recall? *Testing: authentic assessment skill.*

## 3. Working with faculty (26–40)

26. **C** How do you build rapport with faculty? *Testing: relationship skills.*
27. **C** Tell us about a time you worked with someone resistant to change. *Testing: patience and strategy.*
28. **B** A senior faculty member says, "I've taught this way for 25 years, and it works." How do you respond? *Testing: respect plus evidence.*
29. **C** How do you handle disagreements with a subject matter expert? *Testing: knowing whose call it is.*
30. **B** Who owns the course, you or the faculty member? *Testing: understanding academic freedom.*
31. **C** How do you manage multiple faculty projects at once? *Testing: organization and prioritization.*
32. **B** How would you work with adjunct faculty who have little time and little pay for course development? *Testing: empathy and realistic scope.*
33. **C** How do you give feedback on a course without making faculty feel judged? *Testing: tact.*
34. **B** A faculty member wants a flashy tool that doesn't serve the outcome. What do you do? *Testing: steering back to alignment.*
35. **C** Tell us about a time you had to explain a technical concept to a non-technical audience. *Testing: communication.*
36. **B** How do you know when a consultation went well? *Testing: outcome-focused thinking.*
37. **C** How do you handle a faculty member who misses deadlines? *Testing: accountability without conflict.*
38. **B** How would you get faculty to attend programming they don't think they need? *Testing: motivation and diffusion.*
39. **C** Describe a successful collaboration you led. *Testing: a concrete story with results.*
40. **B** How do you work with faculty across very different disciplines? *Testing: learning outside your expertise.*

## 4. Learning science and pedagogy (41–52)

41. **C** What learning theories guide your work? *Testing: grounding, not name-dropping.*
42. **B** Which learning science finding do you wish every faculty member used? *Testing: conviction and evidence.*
43. **C** How do you promote active learning? *Testing: practical strategies.*
44. **C** How do you increase student engagement in online courses? *Testing: presence and interaction.*
45. **B** How do you apply cognitive load theory to course materials? *Testing: applied knowledge.*
46. **C** What is Universal Design for Learning, and how do you apply it? *Testing: principles in practice.*
47. **B** How do you design for first-generation or underprepared students? *Testing: equity at a state college.*
48. **C** How do you design effective feedback into a course? *Testing: formative assessment.*
49. **B** How would you help faculty build retrieval practice and spacing into an existing course? *Testing: low-effort, high-impact changes.*
50. **C** How do you support student motivation? *Testing: relevance, autonomy, belonging.*
51. **B** What does your experience in a learning lab show you about where courses fail students? *Testing: your unique perspective.*
52. **C** What's the difference between andragogy and pedagogy, and does it matter here? *Testing: adult learners at a state college.*

## 5. Assessment and evaluation (53–62)

53. **C** How do you evaluate whether a course is effective? *Testing: evidence beyond satisfaction surveys.*
54. **C** How do you design rubrics? *Testing: clear criteria.*
55. **B** How would you measure the impact of the Backward Design Academy on students? *Testing: program evaluation thinking.*
56. **C** What evaluation models do you know? *Testing: Kirkpatrick, Guskey.*
57. **B** Course evaluations are positive, but DFW rates are high. What do you investigate? *Testing: data reasoning.*
58. **C** How do you use learning analytics or LMS data? *Testing: practical data use.*
59. **B** How do you balance formative and summative assessment? *Testing: assessment design.*
60. **C** How do you revise a course after it runs? *Testing: continuous improvement.*
61. **B** How would you design a pilot to test whether a course change worked? *Testing: basic study design.*
62. **C** How do you gather student feedback on course design? *Testing: student voice.*

## 6. Technology and Canvas (63–72)

63. **C** What's your experience with Canvas? *Testing: hands-on LMS skill.*
64. **C** What authoring tools and technologies do you use? *Testing: current toolset.*
65. **B** How do you decide whether a new tool is worth adopting? *Testing: outcomes before tools.*
66. **C** Tell us about a course or module you built from start to finish. *Testing: a complete portfolio story.*
67. **C** How do you create or edit instructional video? *Testing: media production basics.*
68. **B** How do you keep course materials maintainable after you're no longer involved? *Testing: sustainability.*
69. **C** How do you stay current with educational technology? *Testing: professional learning habits.*
70. **B** When would you build a custom interactive instead of using an existing tool? *Testing: build-versus-buy judgment.*
71. **C** How do you organize a Canvas course for easy navigation? *Testing: usability.*
72. **B** What's your view of open educational resources? *Testing: cost, quality, and adoption tradeoffs.*

## 7. Accessibility and compliance (73–78)

73. **C** How do you ensure courses are accessible? *Testing: concrete practices.*
74. **C** What accessibility standards do you follow? *Testing: WCAG 2.1 AA and ADA.*
75. **B** How would you help faculty fix an inaccessible course with a large backlog of materials? *Testing: prioritizing remediation.*
76. **C** How do you handle copyright and fair use in course materials? *Testing: awareness and referral.*
77. **B** What's the difference between an accessible course and one designed with UDL? *Testing: depth.*
78. **C** What do you know about FERPA as it relates to course design? *Testing: privacy basics.*

## 8. AI (79–87)

79. **C** How do you see AI affecting instructional design? *Testing: balanced, informed view.*
80. **B** A faculty member wants to ban AI completely. How do you help them? *Testing: respect plus practical options.*
81. **B** A faculty member wants to use AI detectors to catch cheating. What do you say? *Testing: evidence and ethics.*
82. **C** How do you use AI in your own design work? *Testing: responsible, verified use.*
83. **B** How should assessment change now that students have AI? *Testing: AI-resilient design.*
84. **B** How would you design an assignment that teaches students to use AI well? *Testing: AI literacy as an outcome.*
85. **C** What concerns do you have about AI in education? *Testing: critical thinking.*
86. **B** How would you help the CTLE develop AI guidance faculty will actually use? *Testing: policy that fits practice.*
87. **B** Tell us about your work with the SLC AI Work Group. *Testing: leadership with evidence of results.*

## 9. Behavioral and situational (88–96)

88. **C** Tell us about a time a project didn't go as planned. *Testing: accountability and learning.*
89. **C** Tell us about a time you received critical feedback. *Testing: coachability.*
90. **C** Describe a time you had competing deadlines. *Testing: prioritization.*
91. **B** Tell us about a decision you made using data. *Testing: evidence-based judgment.*
92. **C** Tell us about a time you took initiative without being asked. *Testing: proactivity within limits.*
93. **B** Tell us about a time you changed your mind. *Testing: intellectual honesty.*
94. **C** How do you handle ambiguity in a project? *Testing: clarifying questions.*
95. **B** Your supervisor asks for a project approach you believe is weaker. What do you do? *Testing: disagree respectfully, then commit.*
96. **C** Describe a time you worked on a team with conflicting priorities. *Testing: collaboration.*

## 10. Closing (97–100)

97. **B** How would you contribute to the CTLE beyond course design? *Testing: workshops, programming, and scholarship.*
98. **C** Where do you see yourself in five years? *Testing: commitment to the role.*
99. **B** Is there anything we haven't asked that you want us to know? *Testing: a strong closing point.*
100. **C** What questions do you have for us? *Testing: curiosity and preparation.*
